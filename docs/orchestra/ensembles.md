## 0. Scope and tags

Scope: how Sonara could know which instruments make up an ensemble, in what order and under what
names they appear, where they sit, and how a template could be applied through today's agent
tools. Instrument ids are those of [model.md](model.md); every id used below exists there, and
the parts no id covers are marked **gap**. No pitch range is repeated here; the cards and
[model.md](model.md) hold them. Behaviour of Sonara's sampler is cited from
[sfz-conformance.md](sfz-conformance.md), which verified it in the code. Tags: [Cited Sn] = a row
of the Sources table above, opened when this research was done; `Card cards/<file>.md#<id>` = a
statement taken from that research card (`#1`, `#2.3` name a numbered section of a card without
ids); [Estimate: reason] = this author's judgement or a design
proposal.

## 1. Score order and part naming

### 1.1 The standard order of a full score

Widor, 1906, states the order outright [Cited S3]: "the order in which the instruments are
usually arranged in orchestral scores has been followed, beginning with the most acute-toned".
His sequence is [Cited S3]:

1. Flutes (piccolo), oboes (cor anglais), clarinets (bass clarinet), bassoons (double bassoon),
   sarrusophone.
2. Horns, trumpets (cornets à pistons), trombones, tubas (saxhorns).
3. The percussion instruments.
4. "So to speak, in parentheses": the saxophones, the harp, the organ.
5. "And lastly", the strings.

Modern teaching sources agree on the frame and fill in the slots Widor leaves open:

| Position (top to bottom) | Content | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Woodwinds, high to low within the family | [Cited S3, S9, S11] |
| 2 | Horns at the top of the brass, above the trumpets, although they sound lower | [Cited S3, S9, S11]; Barnett explains it as horns often being treated as woodwinds [Cited S11]; Wagner placed the horns among the woodwinds [Card cards/horns.md#horn_f] |
| 3 | Trumpets, trombones, tuba | [Cited S3, S9] |
| 4 | Timpani, first within the percussion group, then untuned, then tuned percussion | [Cited S12] |
| 5 | Harp, piano, celesta and other keyboards, after the percussion and before the strings | [Cited S9, S12]; Widor groups harp and organ there too [Cited S3] |
| 6 | Vocal soloists, then chorus, between the percussion block and the strings | [Cited S11] |
| 7 | Strings: Violins I, Violins II, violas, cellos, double basses | [Cited S2, S9] |

- Hugill reduces the order to woodwind, brass, percussion, others, strings, and adds that the
  exact position of single instruments varies by composer and period [Cited S10].
- Within the woodwind family the doubling instruments sit next to their family. Rimsky-Korsakov's
  formation table lists piccolo, flutes, bass flute, oboes, English horn, small clarinet,
  clarinets, bass clarinet, bassoons, double bassoon, in that order [Cited S1] (a formation list,
  read here as staff order [Estimate]).
- **Rule used by the templates of section 3** [Estimate: design choice that reconciles the
  formation list above with keeping one player's tracks together]: an auxiliary instrument
  with its own player takes the place of the formation list (piccolo above the flutes, English
  horn below the oboes, E♭ clarinet above the clarinets, bass clarinet and contrabassoon below
  their families); a wind doubling track ("Piccolo (Fl. 3)") sits directly below its player's
  main track. In the harp and keyboard block the order below holds whoever plays the part, so a
  celesta played by the pianist still sits above the piano.
- Wagner tuba staves go directly below the horns when horn players play them
  [Card cards/horns.md#horn_f].
- The relative order of harp, piano and celesta is not settled by the sources read
  [Estimate: S9 lists harp before piano, S12 does not order them]. Proposed default for
  Sonara, used by every template below: harp, celesta, piano, organ [Estimate: design choice;
  see Open questions].
- No opened source orders the keyboard and harp block relative to the voices: S9 and S12 place
  keyboards between percussion and strings, S11 places soloists and chorus there. Rows 5 and 6
  above put keyboards and harp first, then soloists, then chorus [Estimate: common practice,
  unverified; see Open question 2].

### 1.2 Common variants

| Variant | What changes | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| Trumpets before horns | Rimsky-Korsakov's brass formation table lists trumpets before horns, then trombones and tuba [Cited S1] | whether this mirrors Russian score layout is [Estimate: S1 gives a formation list, not a staff order] |
| 17th-century and Baroque scores | built around a string core, winds added irregularly; instruments playing the same line (bassoons with cellos, oboes with violins) may share a staff; the continuo sits below the bass line | [Cited S9] |
| Late 18th century | publishers begin to standardise; winds above strings, high to low; cellos and basses often still share one staff | [Cited S9] |
| Baroque and Classical scores in general | no fixed layout; each must be learned individually | [Cited S11] |
| Classical "Bassi" | cellos and basses read one part, the basses sounding an octave lower | [Cited S5]; [Card cards/strings.md#double_bass] |
| Saxophones | Widor puts them after the percussion with harp and organ [Cited S3]; modern scores usually place them after the clarinets | [Estimate: common modern engraving; no source opened states it] |
| Concert (wind) band | percussion below the other staves rather than above strings | [Estimate: S32 was not readable, so the band order is unverified] |
| Big band | saxophones, trumpets, trombones, then the rhythm section at the bottom | [Cited S12] |

### 1.3 How parts are named and numbered

- **Players versus parts.** Rimsky-Korsakov's legend: "Arabic numerals denote the number of
  players on each instrument; roman figures, the parts (1st, 2nd etc.). Instruments which do not
  require additional players, but are taken over by one or the other executant in place of his
  usual instrument, are enclosed in brackets." [Cited S1]
- **Who doubles.** "As a rule the first flute, first oboe, first clarinet and first bassoon never
  change instruments"; the special instruments are "taken by the second and third players in each
  group" [Cited S1].
- **Flute 3 doubling piccolo.** Rimsky-Korsakov writes "(II--Piccolo)" for woodwind in pairs,
  "(III--Piccolo)" in threes and a separate "1 Piccolo (IV)" in fours [Cited S1]. Forsyth's rule
  of his day: three flutes, the third alternating with piccolo [Card cards/flutes.md#flute].
- **Extras.** An instrument used only for part of a work, needing its own player, is an "extra"
  [Cited S1].
- **Horns 1-4.** Horns work in pairs: 1 and 3 play the upper parts, 2 and 4 the lower, and
  chords are interleaved 1-3-2-4 [Card cards/horns.md#horn_f]. Rimsky-Korsakov writes "4 Horns I, II,
  III, IV" and "6 or 8 Horns" for the largest formation [Cited S1].
- **Unison of two players on one staff.** "a 2" over a wind staff means both players play the
  line [Card cards/high_brass.md#trumpet_bb].
- **Violins I and II.** Forsyth, 1914: "The Violins are for orchestral purposes always divided
  into two main-groups of '1st' and '2nd' Violins, and their parts occupy two lines in the
  score." [Cited S2]
- **Divisi.** Players divide by desks (desks 1, 3, 5 upper; 2, 4, 6 lower) or by seat (one side
  of each desk takes the top line); the score should say how, for example "Vns I, 1, 2, 3 desks,
  6 'Cellos div. à 3" [Cited S1]. Short divisi stays on one staff with stems up and down and
  "div."; for elaborate divisi "it is as well to write not more than two parts on a single
  stave" [Cited S2]. Division into four or more parts is rare and lowers the volume [Cited S1].
- **Labels.** Full instrument names on the first page, abbreviations on later pages [Cited S10].
  The cards carry a `score_abbreviation` per id, for example "Vn." [Card cards/strings.md#violin].
- **Proposed Sonara track names** [Estimate: design proposal, following S1 and S2]:
  - one track per wind or brass part: "Flute 1", "Flute 2", "Horn 3", "Trumpet 1";
  - a doubling instrument gets its own track named after the instrument and the player:
    "Piccolo (Fl. 3)", "English horn (Ob. 2)";
  - one track per string section: "Violins I", "Violins II", "Violas", "Cellos", "Basses";
    optional divisi tracks "Violins I div. 1" and "Violins I div. 2";
  - choir sections "Sopranos", "Altos", "Tenors", "Basses (choir)"; solo voices "Soprano solo";
  - percussion tracks named after the instrument, with the player in brackets:
    "Snare drum (Perc. 1)".

## 2. Seating and what it means for stereo placement

### 2.1 Layouts in use

| Layout | Strings from the conductor's left to right | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| Antiphonal ("German" or "European"), the 18th- and 19th-century norm | Violins I, cellos, violas, Violins II; first and second violins on opposite sides | [Cited S5, S7, S8] |
| American ("Stokowski shift", 1920s Philadelphia; also Henry Wood in England) | Violins I, Violins II, violas, cellos; basses behind the cellos | [Cited S7, S8, S5] |
| Common variation | Violins I, Violins II, cellos, violas | [Cited S7] |
| Pit orchestra | cellos and basses in the middle, violins and violas on the left, winds on the right | [Cited S5] |

- Forsyth, 1914, describes the antiphonal layout as the usual one: "the 2nd Violins, sitting on
  the conductor's right hand, hold their instruments with the sound-holes turned away from the
  audience" [Cited S2]. He judges them dynamically "about equal to half the number of 1st
  Violins" for that reason [Cited S2].
- Germany and Austria did not adopt the Stokowski shift; Mahler and Elgar still wrote for
  antiphonal violins; the Jacksonville orchestra switches to antiphonal seating for classical
  repertoire [Cited S8].
- The Berliner Philharmoniker alternate between layouts; the conductor decides; the basses stand
  behind the strings on the left or the right; the horns can change sides [Cited S7].
- Front to back, sections are arranged by volume: strings, then woodwinds, then brass, with
  percussion at the back [Cited S7].
- Basses usually stand in an arc behind the cellos, in one or two rows [Cited S5].
- Big band: the lead players (first trumpet, first trombone, first alto) sit in the middle of
  their sections, and the solo chairs sit nearest the rhythm section [Cited S13]. Tenor 1 is a
  solo chair beside the rhythm section [Card cards/saxophones.md#saxophone_tenor].

### 2.2 Berlioz: winds, brass, percussion, harps, chorus and soloists

Berlioz is the only source opened that places every group, including the chorus [Cited S4]:

> the first violins in front on the right, facing the public ; the second violins in front on
> the left ; the violas, in the middle, between the two groups of violins ; the flutes,
> hautboys, clarinets, horns, and bassoons behind the first violins ; a double rank of
> violoncellos and double-basses behind the second violins ; the trumpets, cornets, trombones,
> and tubas behind the violas ; the rest of the violoncellos and double-basses behind the wooden
> wind instruments ; the harps in the foreground, close to the orchestral conductor ; the
> kettle-drums, and other instruments of percussion behind or in the centre of the brass
> instruments [Cited S4]

- The chorus stands on a floor in front of the orchestra, fanned towards the public; the women
  (sopranos and contraltos) sit in front, the tenors stand behind the contraltos and the basses
  behind the sopranos; the soloists take the centre front [Cited S4].
- His "right" and "left" are given "facing the public", so it is unclear whether they are the
  conductor's or the audience's sides [Estimate: wording ambiguous]. This document does not turn
  them into pan signs.
- The same treatise describes "the finest concert orchestra, for a room scarcely larger than
  that of the Conservatoire", with, among others, 21 first violins, 20 seconds, 18 violas,
  8 + 7 cellos, 10 double basses and 4 harps [Cited S4] (the list's layout in the OCR text is
  scrambled, so these counts need a check against a page scan [Estimate]).

### 2.3 Harp, keyboards and choir today

- Modern positions of harp, piano, celesta and choir are not stated in any page opened
  [Estimate: S7 and S8 describe only strings, horns and the front-to-back order].
- Proposed defaults [Estimate: common concert practice, not verified]: harp and keyboards behind
  the first violins on the left; percussion at the back right; timpani at the back centre; choir
  on risers behind the orchestra, sopranos on the left and basses on the right; vocal soloists at
  the front centre, as in Berlioz [Cited S4 for soloists only].

### 2.4 Consequences for a DAW

- **Libraries recorded in situ carry the stage image in their samples.** Spitfire recorded its
  symphonic strings with "60 Players recorded in situ (16, 14, 12, 10, 8)" at AIR Lyndhurst
  [Cited S26]. Spitfire also published the placement used for the whole Spitfire Symphony
  Orchestra recording [Cited S28], but the diagram itself could not be read [Cited S28]. BBC SO
  Discover was recorded at Maida Vale Studios; its page does not say how players were placed
  [Cited S27].
- **None of those libraries plays in Sonara today.** Spitfire's libraries and BBC SO Discover
  run only in their makers' plugins, which Sonara cannot host before plugin hosting (milestone
  M5); Sonara's sampler plays SFZ [Card cards/free_libraries.md#bbc_so_discover]
  [Card cards/strings.md#violin] [Cited S31: ADR 0010]. The in-situ evidence
  above therefore describes a later plugin route, not the free SFZ libraries Sonara can load now.
- **Placement written as SFZ opcodes is lost.** The sampler ignores the SFZ `pan` and `width`
  opcodes ([sfz-conformance.md](sfz-conformance.md), Q9). A stage image recorded into a stereo
  sample survives; a library that places its sections with those opcodes arrives centred
  [Estimate: consequence of Q9; no library was found that does this].
- **Not every library states its positioning.** The Hollywood Strings review describes close
  microphones "directly in front of the players" and divisi microphones left and right of each
  section, but says nothing about stage positions [Cited S29].
- **Rule proposed for Sonara** [Estimate: design proposal]:
  - a library marked as positioned in situ keeps every track at pan 0, and the template's pan
    table is not applied;
  - a dry or centred source (the Poly Synth, a solo-instrument SFZ, an unknown library) gets
    the template's pan table below;
  - the pan is set on the track mixer (`set_track_pan`, −1 left to +1 right [Cited S31]), not
    on the sampler's own `pan` parameter, so the library's own pan stays untouched;
  - the library manifest would need a field such as `positioned: true` to know which case
    applies: `sonara-library.json` v1 has only `id`, `name`, `license` and per-patch
    `path`, `name`, `family` and `articulations` [Cited S31:
    `crates/sonara-app/src/instruments/manifest.rs`].
- One tutorial places the violins left of centre, the cellos right of centre and the basses in
  the centre, and handles depth with convolution reverb rather than panning [Cited S30].
- Sonara's folders are for organisation only; grouping with linked faders is still planned
  [Cited S31]. Group processing (a strings bus, a brass bus) therefore needs bus tracks and
  output routing, not folders [Estimate: inference from S31]. Logic separates the two cases in
  the same way: a folder stack groups tracks without changing routing, a summing stack routes
  its subtracks to a bus [Card cards/apple.md#1].

## 3. Ensemble templates

Conventions for every table [Estimate: design proposal]:

- Parts are in score order (section 1, including the rule for auxiliary and doubling
  instruments). "Players" counts people on that part; 0 means the part is played by a player
  counted on another row; a string section is one track with N players.
- "Req." = required for the template; "opt." = optional (an instrument used by only some works,
  or an extra).
- "Player / doubling" names who plays a doubled instrument; the doubling track is never sounding
  at the same time as the player's main part.
- An id marked **gap** has no entry in [model.md](model.md); the template uses the nearest id and
  says so, or falls back to a built-in plugin.
- A track count counts one track per part row, with "1 + 1" rows split into two tracks, one
  track per percussion instrument, and no folder, bus or divisi tracks.
- Only 16 ids exist in `crates/sonara-midi/src/orchestration/data.rs` today; `viola`, `timpani`,
  `tuba`, `harp` and most others are research ids from [model.md](model.md) not yet in data.rs
  [Cited S31].

## 4. A machine-readable template format (proposal)

Everything in this section is a design proposal [Estimate: design proposal; not an existing
Sonara format].

### 4.1 Where it would live

- Pure data next to the instrument metadata in `sonara-midi::orchestration`, validated at compile
  time like the instruments, or as JSON files embedded in `sonara-app` [Estimate: design
  choice]. The instruments are already checked at compile time: `Instrument::new` in
  `crates/sonara-midi/src/orchestration/instrument.rs` is a `const fn` that rejects invalid
  data [Cited S31].
- Not in the `.sonara` file. A template is a recipe; the project keeps only the tracks it
  produced [Estimate: avoids a CTR-01 schema change].
- A validator rejects unknown instrument ids, doubling of a player's two instruments at the same
  moment, and parts whose id has no built-in fallback [Estimate].
- Every part must resolve to a built-in plugin and preset, because ADR 0010 bundles no library
  and `sonara.sampler` is registered only when a configured library holds at least one patch
  [Cited S31: ADR 0010; `crates/sonara-app/src/samples.rs`]. The template does not repeat that
  choice: the built-in for each instrument id is step 6 of the degradation chain in
  [sound-mapping.md](sound-mapping.md) (section 4.3), the one source of truth (DES-01)
  [Estimate: design choice].

Notes on the fields [Estimate: design proposal]:

- `doublers` lists only people who change instrument; a part without `player` has its own
  player, and its `players` field counts the people on it (0 for a doubling). Two parts with
  the same `player` must not sound at the same time, and a change of instrument needs rest time
  (Forsyth advises against changing clarinets in the middle of a piece, because a cold
  instrument plays out of tune [Card cards/clarinets.md#clarinet_a]).
- `short` is the id's score abbreviation from [model.md](model.md) plus the part number; the
  key of a transposing instrument is left out, because the part's instrument id carries it.
- `pan` is shown on a few parts to illustrate the field; a part without `pan` takes the default
  of 2.5, and `pan` is ignored for libraries positioned in situ (2.4). `send_db` is likewise
  shown on one part only; a real template gives every part its send.
- `alternatives` lets a work pick `clarinet_a` instead of `clarinet_bb` without a second
  template.
- The return bus sets the reverb's `mix` to 1.0, as the existing templates do for their
  "Reverb" bus [Cited S31: `crates/sonara-app/src/templates.rs`]. "Concert Hall" is a 2.8 s
  hall preset of `sonara.reverb` [Cited S31: `crates/sonara-core/src/plugins/builtin.rs`].
- There is no `fallbacks` block: each part's built-in comes from step 6 of
  [sound-mapping.md](sound-mapping.md) by instrument id (4.1). For this roster that means Poly
  Synth "Strings" and "Brass" for strings and brass, "Warm Pad" or "Square Lead" for the
  woodwinds (Poly Synth has no woodwind preset), "Pluck" for the harp and the Drum Kit for the
  percussion. The Drum Kit cannot stand in for every part: timpani lose their pitch, and only
  notes 41, 43, 45, 47, 48 and 50 are toms (42, 44 and 46 are hi-hats, 49 a crash); GM triangle
  notes 80 and 81 play nothing, so a triangle part (3.2) has no Drum Kit sound; the tam-tam has
  no GM note and is stood in for by the crash on 49
  ([sfz-conformance.md](sfz-conformance.md), C20 and C22; [model.md](model.md), GM column;
  [sound-mapping.md](sound-mapping.md), 4.3).

### 4.3 Applying a template without a schema change

`create_project` has a fixed enum `"empty" | "electronic" | "band" | "orchestral"` in its
schema, hand-written in the agent contract and pinned by the golden tool list [Cited S31]. A new
template id there is a CTR-01 change. A template can still be applied after `create_project`
(with `template: "empty"`) as a sequence of existing tools [Cited S31 for the tools and their
parameters; Estimate for the sequence]:

1. For each folder: `create_track {kind: "folder", name: "Woodwinds"}`.
2. For each reverb or family bus: `create_track {kind: "bus", name: "Hall"}`, then
   `add_effect {track_id, plugin_id: "sonara.reverb", preset, params: {mix: 1.0}}`.
3. For each part: `create_track {kind: "instrument", name: "Flute 1", instrument: <plugin_id>,
   preset: <preset>, color, parent_id: <folder id>, output: "master" or a bus id}`.
4. When a library patch is installed for the part's id: `set_instrument {track_id, plugin_id:
   "sonara.sampler", params: {instrument: <patch path>}}`, with the path exactly as the
   sampler's `instrument` enum lists it; otherwise keep the built-in (ADR 0010 [Cited S31]).
   - Which patch fits an id is not known to Sonara today: the library manifest has no field that
     maps a patch to an instrument id [Cited S31: `crates/sonara-app/src/instruments/manifest.rs`];
     [sound-mapping.md](sound-mapping.md) proposes that mapping.
   - The enum's choices are the configured library root joined with the patch path, not
     canonicalized, so they are absolute only when the configured root is
     ([sfz-conformance.md](sfz-conformance.md), C1).
   - A missing or unloadable patch renders as silence with a warning, and with no library
     configured the device is bypassed; the render does not fail (C3 there).
   - The `instrument` parameter cannot be automated (C2 there), so a player's doubling cannot
     switch patch on one track. This is why a doubling gets its own track (Open question 7).
5. `set_track_pan {track_id, pan}` for unpositioned sources (2.4, 2.5).
6. `configure_routing {track_id, add_sends: [{target: <bus id>, level_db}]}`.
7. `set_track_volume` only where the template states a balance offset.

Who can run it today:

- The AI Composer, a headless Claude Code run that uses the `sonara` MCP server [Cited S31:
  `docs/ai-composer.md`].
- The deterministic planner in `sonara_agent::orchestration`, a library API that runs plans of
  existing tools with dry-run validation and a confirmation gate [Cited S31]; "apply template X"
  would be a new plan kind, not a new tool [Estimate].
- The GUI New Project dialog lists `TEMPLATES` from `sonara-app` [Cited S31], so a new entry
  there appears in the GUI without a tool-schema change; the agent still cannot choose it,
  because its enum is a separate literal [Cited S31]. That asymmetry needs an owner decision
  [Estimate].

Limits of the no-schema route:

- Project v1 tracks have no field for an instrument id, a part or an ensemble [Cited S31:
  `docs/project-format.md`, track fields]. The part identity survives only in the track name
  ("Flute 1") and in the template file [Estimate].
- Unknown keys on track objects are preserved round-trip in an `extra` map [Cited S31:
  `docs/project-format.md`], but `create_track` has no parameter for them [Cited S31:
  `golden/contracts/tools.json`], so an `extra.part` key is not reachable by the agent
  [Estimate: inference].
- A large template is many calls, each a separate tool call through permissions and the
  audit log: the late-Romantic template (3.4) has 49 to 70 part tracks, and with
  `create_track`, `set_track_pan` and a send per part that is 150 or more calls
  [Estimate: row counts of 3.4, about three calls per part].

What a versioned schema change would add (CTR-01) [Estimate: design proposal]:

- a track `part` object in `project-v1.schema.json` (`instrument_id`, `part`, `player`,
  `ensemble_id`), written by `create_track` and read by range and doubling checks;
- either a wider `create_project.template` enum or a new `apply_ensemble` tool taking a template
  id, a string-size preset and a seating, returning the created track ids;
- a `list_ensembles` read tool so an agent can discover templates instead of knowing ids.

## 5. Critique of Sonara's current "orchestral" template

What it builds [Cited S31: `templates.rs`]: four instrument tracks named "Strings" (Poly Synth
preset "Strings"), "Brass" (Poly Synth "Brass"), "Choir" (Poly Synth "Choir Pad") and
"Percussion" (Drum Kit "Cinematic Toms"); a "Reverb" bus with the "Cathedral" preset at full
mix; post-fader sends at −6, −8, −5 and −9 dB; a master limiter "Master -1 dBTP". No pan or
volume is set, so every track sits at the centre [Cited S31]; the recorded final project of
`golden/workflows/cinematic_orchestral` confirms pan 0 on every track [Cited S31].

Against the templates of section 3:

| Aspect | Current template | Compared with section 3 | Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Woodwinds | none | every orchestral template has at least a flute and paired oboes and bassoons | [Cited S17, S18, S21] |
| Strings | one pad for the whole section | five sections in every orchestral template; Violins I and II always on separate staves | [Cited S2, S16] |
| Brass | one pad | horns are a separate family from trumpets, trombones and tuba, and sit above them in the score | [Cited S3, S11] |
| Timpani | none; "Cinematic Toms" stand in, and on the Drum Kit only notes 41, 43, 45, 47, 48 and 50 are toms (42, 44, 46 hi-hats, 49 a crash), so no timpani pitch survives | timpani are a part of every orchestral template (3.1-3.6 and 3.9; optional only in 3.1 and 3.6); only the string orchestra (3.7) and the big band (3.8) have none | [Cited S1, S17-S25]; Drum Kit notes: [sfz-conformance.md](sfz-conformance.md), C22 |
| Drum mapping | Drum Kit notes | the `cinematic` style's kick and tom notes would sound as timpani and concert bass drum on a GS orchestra kit | [Card cards/untuned_percussion.md, "Percussion mapping" table] |
| Harp, keyboards | none | harp in 3.3 (optional), 3.4, 3.5 and 3.9; celesta in 3.4 and 3.5; piano in 3.5; harpsichord in 3.6; organ in 3.9 | the rows of section 3, with their own tags; harps [Cited S1, S23, S24, S25] |
| Choir | one pad | four sections, optional soloists | [Cited S1, S25] |
| Score order | Strings, Brass, Choir, Percussion | strings at the top instead of the bottom; no woodwind block | [Cited S3, S9] |
| Seating | everything centred | violins left, low strings right or antiphonal; depth by section | [Cited S7, S8]; values [Estimate] |
| Grouping | all tracks to master, no folders | family folders and optional family buses | [Estimate: design proposal] |
| Reverb | "Cathedral", documented as a 7 s reverb for Ascension; the preset's decay is 7.0 s | a concert hall or scoring stage is shorter than a cathedral; `sonara.reverb` already has "Concert Hall" at 2.8 s, used in 4.2 | [Cited S31: `docs/audio-engine.md`, `crates/sonara-core/src/plugins/builtin.rs`]; comparison [Estimate] |
| Names | "Strings", "Brass" | part names as in 1.3 | [Cited S1, S2] |
| Instrument knowledge | none: synth presets, no orchestration ids | each part maps to an id with ranges and articulations | [Cited S31: `templates.rs`, `data.rs`] |

Constraints that decide what to do with it:

- **The template is golden-covered.** The example `examples/cinematic_orchestral.json`
  (Ascension) starts with `create_project` and `template: "orchestral"`, and its recorded run is
  golden data in `golden/workflows/cinematic_orchestral` (commands, transcript, snapshots, final
  project, MIDI, render) [Cited S31]. Four tests read it [Cited S31]:
  - `crates/sonara-agent/tests/golden_transcripts.rs` replays every step and requires the
    project to hash to the recorded snapshot after each one, so the first step,
    `create_project` with this template, pins the template's output by itself;
  - `crates/sonara-engine/tests/golden_render.rs` null-tests the render against the recording
    (threshold −120 dBFS; measured residual −138.5 dBFS);
  - `apps/sonara/tests/commands/workflows.rs` runs the workflow through the CLI;
  - `crates/sonara-agent/src/state.rs` checks the key order of the final project.
- **Even the track order is load-bearing.** The example sends its "Horn Melody" track to
  `{{project.state.tracks[4].track_id}}`, the Reverb bus as the fifth track the template
  creates [Cited S31: `examples/cinematic_orchestral.json`, `final.project.json`]. Adding,
  removing or reordering template tracks would retarget that send.
- Changing the template's command list in any way would therefore change golden output, and
  GLD-01 forbids re-recording it [Cited S31; GLD-01 in `.claude/rules/project.md`]. New
  ensembles must be added beside `orchestral`, never swapped in.
- Ascension, built from this template, is a render-load baseline in `docs/audio-engine.md`
  (PERF-10) [Cited S31].
- Recommendation [Estimate, following from the constraints above]: keep `orchestral`
  byte-identical as the "cinematic sketch"
  starting point, and add the ensembles of section 3 as data, applied by the planner or the AI
  Composer through existing tools (4.3); widen the `create_project` enum only by a versioned
  CTR-01 decision.
- What the current template does well [Estimate]: it starts quickly with no library installed,
  renders cheaply, and its family split (strings, brass, choir, percussion) matches how
  GarageBand-style users think; the new templates should keep that by offering a "sketch"
  string-size preset with one track per family.

## Open questions

1. **Classical-era section sizes.** No opened source gives Haydn or Mozart string counts. The
   Jacksonville page speaks of an Esterházy orchestra of about 40 [Cited S8]; search snippets
   (pages not opened: a Henle preface, a Ravinia programme note, a review) gave about 25 at
   Esterháza and about 60 in London. The 8-6-4-3-2 default of 3.1 is an Estimate until a primary
   source (Mozart's letters, Haydn scholarship) is read.
2. **Harp, celesta and piano order**, and the order of that keyboard block relative to vocal
   soloists and chorus, are not settled by the sources read; the Dorico manual (S32) could not
   be opened. Decide the defaults after checking a style guide.
3. **Berlioz's "right" and "left"** are stated "facing the public"; whose side is meant needs a
   reading of the French original before any pan value is derived from S4.
4. **Modern harp, keyboard and choir positions** and every pan value in 2.5 are Estimates; a
   listening test with an in-situ library (Spitfire) against the table would calibrate them.
5. **Which libraries are positioned in situ.** Only Spitfire's string page states it [Cited S26],
   and Spitfire's libraries and BBC SO Discover need plugin hosting before Sonara can play them
   [Card cards/free_libraries.md#bbc_so_discover]. The SFZ libraries Sonara can load (VSCO 2 CE,
   Sonatina, Virtual Playing Orchestra 3) need checking, including whether any of them places
   sections with the `pan` or `width` opcodes that the sampler ignores (2.4), and
   `sonara-library.json` needs a field to record the answer.
6. **Missing ids** found while building the templates: natural horn and natural trumpet, alto
   trombone, E♭ trumpet (the model has `trumpet_bb`, `trumpet_c` and `trumpet_piccolo`), bass
   trumpet, contrabass trombone, C clarinet, cowbells, hammer, drum kit, jazz or electric
   guitar, electric bass. The Rute is not on this list: the cards model it as the proposed
   articulation `rute` of `bass_drum` (3.4). Decide which of the others become new ids in
   [model.md](model.md).
7. **Doubling model.** Should a doubled instrument be its own track (proposed) or an
   articulation-like switch on the player's track? The first fits the sampler (one patch per
   device, and its `instrument` parameter cannot be automated:
   [sfz-conformance.md](sfz-conformance.md), C2); the second matches how a part is printed.
8. **Wind quintet and brass quintet score order** (horn position) is an Estimate.
9. **Film orchestra roster** has no cited source beyond library rosters; an orchestration
   handbook chapter on studio orchestras or a published session layout should be read.
10. **Template exposure.** GUI-only templates in `TEMPLATES` versus a versioned extension of the
    `create_project` enum: an owner decision under CTR-01.
11. **Wagner tuba part numbering** conflicts between sources [Card cards/horns.md#wagner_tuba_tenor];
    templates use "Tenor Wagner tuba 1, 2" and "Bass Wagner tuba 1, 2" until resolved.
12. **Alto trombone after Beethoven's middle period (closed).** The tenor trombone card once said the
    alto was dropped from Beethoven's middle period on; Symphony No. 5 scores alto, tenor and bass
    trombones [Cited S19]. The card has been corrected: it now cites the Fifth (composed 1804-1808) and
    the Ninth as scoring alto, tenor and bass trombones [Card cards/low_brass.md#trombone_tenor]. The
    Beethoven template (3.2) follows the symphony.
