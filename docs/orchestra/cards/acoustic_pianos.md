Conventions used by every card below:

- **Comfortable range.** Every key of a keyboard can be played with the same ease, so `sounding_comfortable`
  equals `sounding_playable` for all six cards [Estimate: no opened source names a narrower "comfortable" piano
  range; register-dependent balance and colour are described in section 4 instead]. See open question 1.
- **The `keyboard` object.** Its strings summarise facts that are tagged in sections 2-5 of the same card; they
  carry no tags of their own. Where a section marks a fact as an Estimate, the summary is an Estimate too.
- **Octave names.** Logic Pro's "Display Middle C as" menu labels MIDI 60 as C3 with the "C3 (Yamaha)" setting,
  which the page calls the standard used by most manufacturers, and as C4 with the "C4 (Roland)" setting
  [Cited S25]; that C3 is Logic's factory default is [Estimate: the page never uses the word "default"]. All note
  names here use C4 = 60.
- **The pedals are controls, not articulations.** MIDI 1.0 defines CC64 "Damper Pedal on/off (Sustain)", CC66
  "Sostenuto On/Off" and CC67 "Soft Pedal On/Off", each with values up to 63 meaning off and 64 or more meaning on,
  and the page says nothing about half-pedal values [Cited S29]. Libraries read CC64 continuously: VSL's half-pedal
  zone runs from value 26 to 102 [Cited S26]. Recommendation: treat CC64 as continuous (0-127) and let the library
  decide the curve [Estimate: S29 and S26 disagree; continuous data is a superset of on/off].

**Action (what makes a grand play differently).** In a grand the action lies under the strings and returns by
gravity [Cited S11]. The repetition lever holds the hammer up after a blow; with the key let up only about a third of
its travel the jack resets, so the same key can repeat without the damper touching the string between notes
[Cited S19]. Typical regulation: hammer-to-string distance (blow) 45-47 mm, let-off 1-3 mm [Cited S19]. The
double-repetition mechanism is not part of the upright action [Cited S19].

**Dampers.** A key lifts its damper; releasing it lets the damper fall back and stop the string [Cited S11]. The top
octave and a half of acoustic pianos is traditionally undamped; Keyscape's "LA Custom C7" has no dampers above F6
[Cited S23]; that this is a customised Yamaha C7 is [Estimate: from the name; S23 does not mention Yamaha]. F6 is
read as MIDI 89 [Estimate: the page does not state its octave convention; 89 leaves 19 undamped
keys up to C8, matching the same page's "top octave and a half", whereas a C3 = 60 reading would leave only 7]. The
exact first undamped key differs between makes [Estimate: S22 and S23 give no common value].

**Two-stage decay.** The tone first decays fast (prompt sound, about 8 dB/s "in this case", i.e. Weinreich's measured
example), then slowly (aftersound, under a quarter of that rate) [Cited S18]. The aftersound comes from the two or three unison strings struck by one hammer: their
in-phase motion drives the bridge hard and decays fast, the anti-phase motion barely moves the bridge and lasts
[Cited S18]. Stopping one string of a pair made the remaining one jump up by close to 20 dB [Cited S18].

**Extra resonance.** Duplex scaling uses short non-speaking string lengths; Blüthner's aliquot system adds a fourth
string in the upper two treble sections [Cited S11]. The Imperial's extra bass strings add harmonic resonance
[Cited S7].

What a composer writes differently for the piano:

- **Pedalling.** Composers mark where the damper pedal goes down and where it is released ("Ped." and a release sign);
  Berlioz insists the marks are followed [Cited S3]. Under a held pedal, chromatic passing notes in the middle register
  blur into the harmony; in the top register they are safe because the strings die quickly [Cited S3].
- **Una corda / tre corde** mark the soft pedal on and off [Cited S13].
- **Voicing within a chord** (bringing out a melody note, lighter ornaments, softer bass) is the pianist's art and is
  written with dynamics and accents [Cited S3 for the principle]; MIDI expresses it only through per-note velocity
  [Estimate: MIDI 1.0 has one velocity per note-on].

### 6. Solo vs section (typical number of players, divisi, unison doubling)

- One player, or two at one instrument "à quatre mains"; Berlioz describes an orchestral four-hand part [Cited S3].
- Berlioz imagines many grand pianos together, like harps, but notes that a dozen would not fit a moderate orchestra
  [Cited S3]. Sections of pianos are otherwise not an orchestral norm [Estimate].
- `solo_or_section`: solo [Estimate: one player per instrument; no divisi concept].

### 7. Role in the orchestra and typical doublings

- Rimsky-Korsakov: the piano as an orchestral (non-solo) instrument belongs almost entirely to the Russian school,
  imitating the gusli (with the harp) or soft bells; when it is part of the orchestra, an upright is preferable to a
  grand [Cited S1].
- Forsyth: as a soloist it ranks highest, but in the orchestra it has served only as a substitute for the harp or
  celesta, or to fill out the harmony in a small band; he cites Strauss's *Ariadne auf Naxos* and Scriabin's
  *Prometheus* as attempts to use it as an orchestral instrument [Cited S2].
- Berlioz praises the high, slow piano figures over winds in Beethoven's Eb concerto [Cited S3].
- Typical doublings: harp, celesta, glockenspiel and pizzicato strings for attack; low strings for bass octaves
  [Estimate: general practice; S2 names the harp and celesta substitutions].
- Film and media: a concert grand often carries a solo theme over strings [Estimate].

## Open questions and disagreements between sources

1. **Comfortable range for keyboards.** No source defines one; all six cards set comfortable = playable (Estimate).
   Alternative: `null`. Sonara's range check would then never warn on a piano. Decide once for every keyboard card.
2. **Concert vs studio grand boundary.** S11 starts the concert grand at 2.2 m, S41 and S43 at 240 cm; S42 says
   concert grands "vary between 2 and 3 metres" (a range, not a threshold) and that the French names follow no
   standard. Recommendation: concert grand 2.4 m and longer (Steinway D 274 cm, S4;
   Bösendorfer Imperial 290 cm, S6), studio grand about 1.8-2.4 m (S41's Studioflügel plus Halbkonzertflügel).
3. **Bösendorfer Imperial width.** S6 shows 168 cm only as an unlabelled number in its silhouette diagrams, so a
   maker width of 168 cm is [Estimate: reading of the diagram]; S7 states 175 cm. Neither value affects Sonara's
   data; record both and prefer a maker statement if one is found. Range:
   keep `piano_concert_grand` at 21-108 and treat the Imperial's 12-108 as a variant; Sonara must decide whether a
   97-key variant needs its own id (and whether notes 12-20 should pass the range check for it).
4. **Prepared-piano pitch semantics.** The key range is 21-108, but prepared keys may sound no recognisable pitch
   (S14, S15). The schema has no field for "pitched key, unreliable pitch"; `pitched: true` was kept.
5. **Una corda: one or two strings.** Berlioz says the soft pedal leaves one of three strings and reduces the sound
   by two thirds (S3, a 19th-century description); modern grands strike two of three (S12, S13). Recommend the
   modern description; the volume claim is unmeasured.
6. **CC64 on/off vs continuous.** MIDI 1.0 defines on/off at 63/64 (S29); VSL reads half-pedal values 26-102 (S26);
   Wikipedia says only better digital pedals send half-pedal data (S12). Recommend continuous CC64, library-defined.
7. **Soft pedal on an upright.** CC67 is "Soft Pedal" (S29), but on an upright it is a half-blow (closer hammers,
   no timbre change: S11, S13), not an una corda. Mapping it to CC67 is an Estimate. The practice/muffler pedal and
   Fazioli's fourth pedal have no standard CC (VSL uses CC75 for the fourth pedal, S26).
8. **Double escapement in uprights.** S19 says the double-repetition mechanism is not in the upright action. Search
   snippets (not opened) claimed some C. Bechstein and Sauter uprights have repetition actions. Unverified.
9. **First undamped key.** Keyscape's "LA Custom C7" (a Yamaha C7 only by Estimate): none above F6 (S23; read as 89
   because the page does not state its octave convention and only 89 matches its "top octave and a half"); "top
   octave and a half" (S23); "top couple of octaves" on uprights, and on grands too (S22, no make named). A forum snippet (not opened) claimed 21 undamped notes from E6 on Steinways.
   The value is per model.
10. **Honky-tonk detune amount.** No opened source gives cents; 5-15 cents is an Estimate. S18 only gives the 0.3 Hz
    lock threshold.
11. **GM mapping.** Studio grand mapped to program 2 (Wikipedia: "based on a Yamaha C7", S27) rather than 1: a Sonara
    choice. Upright, felt and prepared piano have no GM1 program; fallback program 1 is an Estimate. Wikipedia's
    model attributions for programs 1 and 2 could not be checked against the spec (S30 has no table); the program
    numbers and names 1-8 themselves are confirmed by a second source, MakeMusic's Finale manual (S46).
12. **Logic's "Steinway Grand Piano".** The task mentions a Logic Steinway Grand Piano patch; no opened Apple page
    names it. Only Studio Piano (Studio Grand, Concert Grand, Vintage Upright, Studio Grand (Mono Mic)) is documented
    (S24).
13. **Fazioli F308 data.** The maker page (S8) states no length or key count in its text; 308 cm and 88 keys are
    Estimates.
14. **Salamander in the Sonara sampler.** The V3 SFZ needs SFZ v2 with ARIA extensions (S32) and uses opcodes that
    `docs/sample-libraries.md` does not list (`#define`, `#include`, `locc`/`hicc`, `on_locc64`, `set_hdcc`,
    `*_oncc`). Test before relying on it; the CC64 pedal-noise samples in particular may never fire.
15. **Vocabulary.** New ids outside the preferred list: `cluster` (adjacent keys struck together with palm, fist or
    forearm) and `muted_string` (key struck while a finger presses the string). `sustain` is used for the ordinary
    piano stroke so that default patches match. These need the vocabulary owner's approval.
16. **Italian pedal names.** The Italian article (S43) was served as a long page; the pedal names "pedale di
    risonanza", "una corda" and "pedale tonale (o sostenuto)" were read, but the abbreviations reported with them
    (3C, 1C) look unreliable and are not used.
17. **Older and saloon uprights** may have 85 keys (A0-A7 = 21-105). No opened source states it; the honky-tonk and
    upright cards keep 21-108.
18. **Felt piano identity.** Whether `piano_felt` should be its own id or a "muffler on" state of `piano_upright` is
    a modelling choice. Libraries treat it as a separate instrument (S35, S36, S37); physically it is the same piano
    with a felt rail (S11, S12).
